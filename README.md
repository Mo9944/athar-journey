# Athar Journey

Athar Platform — Phase 1 (Two Connected Parts)



Build a modern, Arabic-first, responsive web platform called Athar (أثر). The platform helps users understand themselves, expand their knowledge, develop their thinking, and turn what they learn into practical action.



1. Remove Authentication Completely



- Remove login, signup, password reset, and authentication screens.

- Let users enter the platform directly through a welcome page.

- Do not require an account or personal identification.

- Allow users to save their progress locally in the browser where possible.

- Include a clear option to reset or delete locally saved data.

- Do not collect unnecessary personal information.



2. Visual Identity and UI/UX



Create a visually attractive, calming, modern, and professional interface.



Color palette



- Deep teal: "#176B68"

- Soft turquoise: "#58BDB0"

- Warm ivory background: "#FAF8F2"

- Light sky blue: "#E8F3FA"

- Soft lavender: "#EEE9F8"

- Muted gold: "#E6BC67"

- Dark navy text: "#243447"

- White cards with subtle shadows



Use the colors harmoniously. Keep the interface clean, with sufficient whitespace, rounded cards, elegant icons, readable Arabic typography, subtle animations, and clear visual hierarchy.



Design requirements



- Arabic-first interface with full RTL support.

- Responsive on mobile, tablet, and desktop.

- Simple navigation with no overwhelming menus.

- Use a consistent sidebar on desktop and bottom navigation on mobile.

- Use progress indicators, visual cards, and clear calls to action.

- Avoid excessive features on the home screen.

- Use a warm, human-centered design that feels welcoming and encourages curiosity.



3. Divide Phase One into Two Connected Parts



The first phase must be organized into two sequential parts, with a clear transition between them.



PART ONE: Self-Discovery and Awareness



Create a dedicated section titled اكتشف ذاتك.



A. Welcome and Introduction



- Explain the purpose of self-discovery in simple Arabic.

- Introduce the user to the self-discovery journey.

- Let users start with the O*NET Interest Profiler.

- Explain that the test explores interests, not fixed abilities, personality diagnoses, or guaranteed career outcomes.



B. O*NET Interest Profiler Integration



Use this official external test link:



https://onetinterestprofiler.org/p/activities



Requirements:



- Add a clear button titled "ابدأ اختبار اكتشاف اهتماماتك".

- Open the test in a new browser tab.

- Explain that the user should return to Athar after completing it.

- Create a result-entry screen where the user can manually enter or select the results they received.

- Provide clear instructions for entering the six RIASEC interest areas:

  - Realistic (R) — واقعي

  - Investigative (I) — بحثي

  - Artistic (A) — فني

  - Social (S) — اجتماعي

  - Enterprising (E) — ريادي

  - Conventional (C) — تقليدي / تنظيمي

- Allow the user to enter scores if the test provides them, or select their reported top interest types if exact scores are unavailable.

- Do not pretend that the external test is directly connected or that results have been automatically retrieved.

- Clearly distinguish user-entered results from any interpretation generated by Athar.



C. Analyze the Test Results



After the user submits their results, generate a structured, easy-to-understand analysis.



The analysis should include:



1. The user's leading interest areas.

2. An explanation of each reported RIASEC interest type.

3. A comparison of the leading interest areas and how they may complement one another.

4. Examples of activities, learning topics, and work environments related to these interests.

5. Reflective questions to help the user determine whether the interpretation matches their actual experiences.

6. Possible skills to explore and practice based on these interests.

7. Suggested learning activities and small practical experiments.

8. Potential academic and occupational areas to research, without presenting them as guaranteed matches or definitive recommendations.



Use charts to display scores when numerical results are entered. If the user only selects top interest types, display a ranked visual summary based on the user's selections, clearly labeling it as user-reported rather than a measured score.



Never treat the results as a psychological diagnosis, a definitive personality profile, or a final career decision. Explain that interests can change with experience and learning.



D. Additional Self-Discovery Tools



Create simple optional activities for:



- Personal values exploration.

- Identifying perceived strengths and areas for development.

- Learning preferences.

- Personal interests and curiosity.

- Reflection on motivations and meaningful experiences.



Keep these activities short and optional. Do not claim they are scientifically validated assessments unless verified.



E. Personal Reflection Journal



Create a private journal where users can:



- Record thoughts, interests, and discoveries.

- Reflect on the test results.

- Record personal strengths and challenges.

- Write goals and questions for future exploration.

- Save notes locally in the browser.



F. Self-Discovery Summary



Generate a personal summary that combines:



- O*NET interest results entered by the user.

- Self-reported values and strengths.

- Reflections and interests.

- Topics the user wants to explore.



Present this as a flexible self-discovery profile, not a fixed label.



Include a button titled "انتقل إلى رحلة التعلم والتطوير" to move to Part Two.



PART TWO: Knowledge, Thinking, and Personal Development



Create a section titled تعلّم وطوّر أفكارك.



This part should use the user's self-discovery profile, when available, to personalize content and activities.



A. Knowledge and Culture Library



Create a curated library with categories such as:



- Science and general knowledge.

- History and biographies.

- Philosophy and critical thinking.

- Culture and literature.

- Values and ethics.

- Digital and media literacy.

- Learning and personal development.



Each resource card should include:



- Title.

- Short description.

- Category.

- Content type.

- Estimated reading or learning time, when known.

- A source or link when available.



Do not invent books, references, authors, or external links. Mark sample content clearly as demo content.



B. Ideas and Thinking Lab



Create an interactive space for:



- Recording and organizing ideas.

- Critical-thinking exercises.

- Logical reasoning activities.

- Creative thinking challenges.

- Problem-solving exercises.

- Mind maps or simple idea connections.

- Reflective questions about assumptions and evidence.



Allow users to save their ideas locally and revisit them.



C. Personal Development



Provide simple tools for:



- Setting personal goals.

- Creating small learning plans.

- Managing time and priorities.

- Building positive habits.

- Improving focus.

- Reflecting on progress.

- Breaking large goals into manageable tasks.



Avoid overwhelming the user with too many tasks or dashboards.



D. Personalized Learning Plans



Use the self-discovery profile and the user's selected goals to propose a flexible learning plan.



Each plan should include:



- A clear objective.

- Small learning steps.

- Suggested topics and resources.

- Practical activities.

- A progress indicator.

- A review point to adjust the plan.



Explain why each recommendation is suggested and allow the user to edit or reject it.



E. AI Learning Assistant



Create one central AI assistant that can:



- Explain concepts in simple Arabic.

- Help users understand their O*NET interest results.

- Ask reflective questions.

- Help organize thoughts and ideas.

- Suggest learning activities based on user-selected interests.

- Help create realistic personal development plans.

- Summarize user-provided notes.

- Support critical thinking without simply giving final answers.



The assistant must not fabricate references or claim that generated interpretations are certain. When external AI credentials are unavailable, show a clearly labeled demo mode rather than pretending that AI functionality is active.



Keep any AI API keys on the server side. Never expose secret keys in client-side code.



F. Progress Dashboard



Create a simple personal dashboard showing:



- Self-discovery completion.

- Saved interests and reflections.

- Learning goals.

- Completed activities.

- Reading and learning progress.

- Ideas saved in the thinking lab.

- Habits and tasks.

- Suggested next steps.



Use clear visual progress indicators and avoid competitive comparisons with other users.



4. Connect Both Parts



The two parts must work as one continuous journey:



Self-discovery → Interest analysis → Reflection → Learning goals → Knowledge and thinking activities → Practical development → Progress review.



Use the user's entered interests and selected goals to personalize learning suggestions in Part Two.



Allow the user to revisit and update their self-discovery profile at any time.



Do not lock the second part if the user skips the O*NET test. They should still be able to explore the platform and set their own goals.



5. Data and Technical Requirements



- Use Supabase only if it is already configured and appropriate; authentication must remain disabled.

- Otherwise, implement local browser persistence for the anonymous user's progress.

- Separate components and features into maintainable modules.

- Provide sensible loading, empty, and error states.

- Ensure accessibility, responsive behavior, and RTL correctness.

- Provide a simple admin/content management area only if it can be safely implemented without public authentication; do not expose private management functions to ordinary visitors.

- Do not claim that an external service, test, AI assistant, or data connection works unless it has actually been integrated and tested.



6. Privacy and Trust



- Explain what information is stored and where.

- Let users clear their saved data.

- Do not collect unnecessary sensitive information.

- Treat self-discovery results as personal reflections and interests, not diagnoses.

- Distinguish test results, user-entered information, and AI-generated interpretations.

- Avoid definitive career claims or promises.



7. Implementation Order



Build and test the platform in this order:



1. Welcome page and anonymous navigation.

2. Main design system, Arabic RTL, and responsive layout.

3. Part One: self-discovery section.

4. External O*NET test link and manual result entry.

5. RIASEC result visualization and analysis.

6. Reflection journal and self-discovery summary.

7. Part Two: knowledge library and ideas lab.

8. Personal development goals and learning plans.

9. AI assistant with secure integration or clearly labeled demo mode.

10. Dashboard, local persistence, privacy controls, and end-to-end testing.



Focus on a polished, easy-to-use MVP. Do not add advanced labor-market monitoring, recruitment, full social networking, subscriptions, or institutional dashboards in this phase.



Final Objective



Athar should feel like a guided journey that helps a person understand their interests, reflect on who they are, discover knowledge, develop their thinking, and turn learning into practical action.



The platform should address both the mind and the human side of development, while keeping the experience simple, visually appealing, transparent, and useful.
